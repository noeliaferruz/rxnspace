Transformation atlas: lab vs enzyme chemistry

Every reaction in two public collections is reduced to its transformation: the atoms whose bonding changes, described independently of the rest of the molecule, so ethanal → ethanol and propanal → propan-1-ol are one transformation. Lab chemistry comes from 3.7 million reaction records text-mined from US patents (1976–2016); enzyme chemistry from the curated Rhea database, with BRENDA (via EnzymeMap) and RetroBioCat as further enzyme sources. The explorer lists the transformations with examples, reagent classes and enzymes; all tables are also JSON and CSV (see Data & API). The report has the results: the size of the space, the overlap, a literature check of the most used lab-only chemistry, what BRENDA and RetroBioCat add, and near misses.

Key numbers

"Enzyme" is Rhea's natural tier (reactions with an annotated enzyme) in the physiological or an unannotated direction. Precedent columns weight by reactions instead of transformations: most enzymatic reactions use a transformation that also occurs in patents, while most distinct transformations of either side do not occur on the other.

What the terms mean

lab
A reaction reported in the experimental section of a US patent grant or application (Lowe's text-mined USPTO set). These are reactions someone describes having carried out, not computer predictions. Patents can contain prophetic examples; a reported yield (55% of records) is the strongest evidence available that the experiment was run, and every count can be restricted to those reactions.
lab only
The transformation occurs in patent reactions and in no Rhea reaction, in either direction. It means that no catalogued natural enzymatic reaction does it. Transformation pages also show what BRENDA (promiscuous activity of natural enzymes) and RetroBioCat (synthetic biocatalysis, public sample only) add; for the 500 most used lab-only transformations, a literature check looked for any protein catalyst.
literature precedent
For a checked lab-only transformation: whether a natural, engineered, designed or artificial enzyme has been reported to do it, and what it needed beyond the 20 canonical amino acids and natural cofactors.
near miss
A lab-only transformation that one edit (another leaving group, one hydrogen more or fewer, aliphatic instead of aromatic, another reactant's point of view) turns into a transformation enzymes do.
enzyme
Rhea reaction with at least one enzyme (UniProt/Swiss-Prot link or EC number), counted once per Rhea master reaction.
Rhea without enzyme
Rhea reactions with no enzyme annotated, including those Rhea marks as able to occur spontaneously, and enzymatic reactions only in their non-physiological direction.
robust
Seen in at least two distinct reactions, or RXNMapper and Indigo atom maps give the same signature. Most singletons are real but some are mapping or text-mining errors.
reagent classes
Families of the agents (reagents, catalysts, solvents) recorded with a patent reaction, e.g. hydride, Pd catalysis, base: the lab "how".
mechanism proxy
EC sub-subclass plus cofactor classes (from the Rhea equation and Swiss-Prot): the enzyme "how", validated against M-CSA curated mechanisms.

Data & API

The site is static: every page reads JSON files that programs can fetch directly (relative to this page). Signatures are 16-hex-digit IDs, stable for a given level definition.
